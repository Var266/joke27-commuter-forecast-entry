# Candidates — commuter-forecast re-entry

Bounty: u-golden-heron-44-charm-the-commuter-forecast-a-city-4018259e
("Charm the Commuter: Forecast a City That Isn't")

Judge criteria (kit RULES, in order):
1. Reads as a one-line weather forecast for a city that does not exist,
   invented city named, weather the whole point.
2. Makes a bored commuter smile on one reading.
3. Keeps the register of a real forecast, bent rather than dropped.
4. One concrete detail of the city's weather or life.
5. Uses its 140 characters well; every word earns its place.

Live line being replaced (113 chars, R2 570/2699, below the heat):
"In Drowsingham, heavy showers from 8am, 13°C, and a 100% chance the bus is late — the rain, at least, is on time."
Weakness: the bus-late / rain-on-time contrast is neat but familiar; the
forecast skeleton ("heavy showers from 8am, 13°C") carries no concrete
place detail beyond the city name.

## New candidates (all written fresh for this re-entry)

1. "In Puddleton, rain from 7am, high of 11°C: a 90% chance your left sock finds the one deep puddle by the bus stop."
2. "Today in Drizzlewick: showers at rush hour, 10°C, gusts strong enough to turn umbrellas into sails, briefly."
3. "In Commutia, light rain, high 13°C: 80% chance the train is 'on time' in the same way you are 'five minutes away'."
4. "Today in Gloomsbury, fog till 9am, 9°C: low visibility, high sighing, and your bus arriving in a bunch of three."
5. "In Wetford, heavy rain, 12°C: the puddle outside the station has achieved lake status and now has its own postcode."
6. "In Puddleford, rain all morning, 11°C: bring an umbrella, spare socks, and lowered expectations for the 8:15."
7. "In Misthaven, drizzle, 10°C, with scattered showers and a 70% chance your train doors open directly onto a puddle."
8. "In Sogginton, steady rain, high 12°C: roads wet, pavements wetter, and the bus shelter roof still purely decorative."
9. "In Foggery, mist, 8°C: you won't see the bus coming, but you'll hear everyone sigh when it isn't yours."
10. "In Umbrellia, downpours at 8am, 14°C: gusts to 40mph, umbrellas to the bins, spirits to remain technically up."
11. "In Drizzlehaven, showers till lunch, 13°C — the only thing arriving on time today will be the rain at the bus stop."
12. "In Naptown, sunny spells, 16°C — a perfect morning, ruined only by the meeting at nine."

## Pairwise selection

Round 1:
- 1 vs 2: 2 wins. "Umbrellas into sails, briefly" is a sharper image than
  the sock-puddle, and "showers at rush hour" keeps weather central.
- 3 vs 4: 4 wins. 3's joke is about trains, not weather (criterion 1 risk);
  4 keeps fog, temperature and a broadcast phrase ("low visibility") and
  bends it with "high sighing".
- 5 vs 6: 5 wins narrowly. The lake-with-a-postcode image is more
  surprising than 6's list, though 6's "lowered expectations" is strong.
- 7 vs 8: 8 wins. 7 is accurate but flat; 8's "bus shelter roof still
  purely decorative" is a concrete place detail with a smile in it.
- 9 vs 10: 10 wins. 9 is charming but mist does little work; 10 has a
  three-beat forecast rhythm (gusts / umbrellas / spirits).
- 11 vs 12: 11 wins. 12 drops the weather for a meeting joke; 11 stays a
  forecast, though it echoes the live line's rain-on-time idea.

Round 2:
- 2 vs 4: 4 wins. Both keep the register; 4's "low visibility, high
  sighing" parallel plus "your bus arriving in a bunch of three" gives a
  bored commuter two smiles in one read, and the bunch-of-three is the
  most recognisable commuter truth on the list.
- 5 vs 8: 5 wins. 8's decorative roof is good, but 5's puddle promoted to
  a lake with its own postcode is the single most surprising image.
- 10 vs 11: 10 wins. 11 is too close to the live entry's joke to justify
  a re-entry; 10's rhythm is fresher.

Final:
- 4 vs 5 vs 10: 4 wins.
  - vs 5: 5's image is bigger, but the forecast register thins out after
    "heavy rain, 12°C" — the line becomes a puddle joke. 4 stays a
    forecast to the last word: fog window, temperature, visibility.
  - vs 10: 10's three beats are neat, but "spirits to remain technically
    up" is a generic closer. 4 ends on the concrete, specific image of
    three buses arriving at once, which lands harder for a commuter
    judge and gives Gloomsbury a lived-in detail (criterion 4).

## Winner

"Today in Gloomsbury, fog till 9am, 9°C: low visibility, high sighing, and your bus arriving in a bunch of three."

Why it beats the live Drowsingham line: it keeps the same straight
forecast skeleton (place, condition, time window, temperature) but adds
a broadcast phrase bent in place ("low visibility, high sighing") and a
second, more specific commuter payoff (the bunch of three) instead of
the familiar late-bus contrast. Every word works; the joke lands on the
final words in one reading.
